**Company:** Halozyme Therapeutics (NASDAQ: HALO)

**Units:** USD millions except per-share amounts  
**Conclusion:** **Watch/defer.** My linked DCF value is **$117.46 per diluted share** versus the saved market price of **$111.56**. The 5.3% difference is not enough compensation for royalty-growth, patent, concentration, leverage, and terminal-value risk.

## As presenter

* **Why is the royalty-growth forecast reasonable?** FY2026 growth of 32.5% is the midpoint of management's then-published 30%–35% outlook. The later path of 22.5%, 17.5%, 12.5%, and 7.5% is my judgment as the current launch cycle matures, not management guidance. The later years remain the biggest research gap.
* **Why rely on a DCF when terminal value is 75.5% of enterprise value?** I treat $117.46 as a conditional estimate, not a precise target. The high terminal-value share is one reason the small premium to market does not support a buy conclusion.
* **Could royalty growth rank first only because its tested range is wider?** Yes. A ±5-point annual royalty-growth test produced a value range of **$99.74–$137.90**, while a ±2-point SG\&A test produced **$113.75–$121.17**. The ranking applies only to those ranges; it is not a probability or proof that growth is always the larger risk.
* **Could the convertible and share-count treatment affect value?** Yes. The model subtracts total debt while using 123.904 million FY2025 diluted weighted-average shares. A better update would model each convertible separately and use a current period-end share count.

**Calculation checked:** In the higher royalty-growth case, FY2030 royalty revenue rises from $2,001.7 million to $2,463.7 million. This increases revenue and gross profit, but also increases SG\&A, R\&D, taxes, capex, and receivables. FY2030 FCFF rises from $1,368.7 million to $1,604.5 million, and value rises from $117.46 to $137.90 per share. The balance-sheet and cash-flow checks pass.
